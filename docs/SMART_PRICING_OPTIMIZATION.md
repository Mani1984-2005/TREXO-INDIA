# Smart Pricing and Discount Optimisation
> Generated by `scripts/retail_generate_docs.py` from actual result files in `reports/ml/retail/` and `models/`.

## Price-response estimate (real data, observational)
Poisson regression of weekly distinct invoices per product on log price with product and week fixed effects (135,063 product-weeks, 2,956 products, 2010-12-01 to 2011-12-09).

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Pooled price coefficient | **-0.736** (95% cluster-bootstrap CI -0.804 to -0.704) |
| First half / second half | -0.598 / -0.671 |
| Placebo (price permuted within product) | 0.004 |
| Usable as pooled prior | True (stable sign, CI excludes 0, placebo ~ 0) |

Interpretation: a 10% price cut is associated with about 8% more purchase events. **This is associational, not causal** (prices were set by the business, possibly in response to demand; wholesaler mix; one UK wholesaler). Product-level elasticities were deliberately not produced (too noisy); a pooled value is used with the conservative CI bound (-0.70) as default. Wholesale quantity tiers make average price and quantity mechanically correlated, which is why the response is purchase events, not units.

## Scenario engine (`pricing/scenarios.py`)
Levels 0/5/10/15/20% (configurable). Demand `q(p) = q0 x min((p/p0)^e, cap)`; sold = min(stock, demand). Outputs: expected demand, units sold, revenue, gross profit, remaining stock, discount cost vs list, units gained vs no discount, `elasticity_source`.

**Objective:** `J = gross_profit - holding_rate x horizon/365 x cost x remaining - obsolescence_rate x cost x remaining`.

**Hard constraints (tested):** discount <= approved maximum; price >= floor = max(approved absolute floor, cost, cost/(1-min_margin)); uplift cap; sold <= stock. A discount is chosen only if J beats full price by more than a configured gain.
**No cost -> analysis only**: revenue/units are shown, profit and selection are withheld. Costs are never invented.

## Key finding
With the validated elasticity (|e| < 1) a discount lowers gross profit at normal margins, so the engine recommends **no discount** in the real-demand replay (see `RETAIL_SIMULATION_REPORT.md`). It only recommends offers when a much stronger price response is assumed. Also, because the model scales *existing* demand, a product with near-zero demand cannot be revived by a modest discount - those products need bundling, visibility, supplier return, liquidation or discontinuation, which the recovery engine proposes instead.

## Controlled offer evaluation
`feedback/store.py`: deterministic hash-based randomisation (`assign_arm`), difference-in-means with bootstrap CI (`analyse_experiment`, refuses to conclude below 20 units per arm). Causal impact of any offer must be measured this way before trusting the elasticity for decisions.
